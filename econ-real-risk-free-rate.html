Real Risk-Free RateThe pure reward for waiting

In one line — the real risk-free rate is the "reward for deferring consumption": the pure interest that remains once inflation and all risk are stripped away.

Part 1Overview — how asset prices work

Every asset's price is the sum of its future cash flows (CF) discounted back to today. This is the present value model.

Price = Σ ( CF cash flow ÷ Discount rate )

The discount rate is the bridge between the economy and asset prices. But that discount rate is itself a build-up of layers, and the real risk-free rate is its ground floor (the foundation).

Discount-rate build-up — the real rate is the foundation

＋ Risk premiumsliquidity · equity · credit · inflation-uncertainty premiums
＋
Expected inflationcompensation for the erosion of money's value
stacked on top of…
Real risk-free ratezero inflation, zero risk → the pure "reward for waiting" · the subject of this note

→ This note covers only the bottom foundation. Expected inflation and the risk premiums are separate lessons.

Markets move on expectations

Markets are forward-looking. Asset prices reflect expectations about the future, not the data that just printed.

  • Anticipated information → already in the price → no change
  • Surprise → expectations get revised → price moves
⚠ Exam trapEven a GDP print that beats last quarter won't move prices if it doesn't change expectations about the future. Only surprises (revisions to expectations) move prices.

Part 2Inter-temporal Rate of Substitution — why interest is demanded

Assumption: NO INFLATION. Strip everything down to time alone.

① Diminishing marginal utility
The consumption–utility curve is concave. Each extra dollar of consumption adds less satisfaction. The first meal when you are hungry 😍; the third meal when you are full 😑.

② Time preference
People prefer consumption today over the future → marginal utility of current consumption > marginal utility of future consumption.

③ So compensation is required
To make someone defer consumption (its opportunity cost) and invest, you must reward them in the future. That reward is the real interest rate.

mₜ (inter-temporal rate of substitution) — the heart of this part

mt = marginal utility of FUTURE consumption ÷ marginal utility of CURRENT consumption

Because today is preferred, the denominator (current) exceeds the numerator (future) → mₜ is always less than 1.

Example: current utility 100, future utility 95 → mₜ = 95/100 = 0.95.

Deriving the real risk-free rate

Step 1. The market price of a 1-year risk-free zero-coupon bond that pays $1 at maturity = expected mₜ. (Investors buy/sell until mₜ = P₀ in equilibrium.)

P0 = E(mt)

Step 2. Return = (amount received − amount paid) ÷ amount paid = 1/P₀ − 1

Rf = (1 − P0) ÷ P0 = 1 ÷ P0 − 1

Step 3. Substitute P₀ = E(mₜ):

Rf = 1 ÷ E(mt) − 1  // mₜ↑ ⟹ Rf↓ (inverse)
  • mₜ = 0.95 → Rf = 1/0.95 − 1 = 5.26%
  • mₜ = 0.90 → Rf = 1/0.90 − 1 = 11.1% (smaller mₜ ⇒ larger Rf ✓)
ⓘ Two relationshipsmₜ ↔ Rf : inverse (mₜ↓ → Rf↑).  Utility of current consumption ↔ Rf : positive (stronger desire to spend today → must pay more to make people defer).

Part 3Economic Factors — two drivers, both positive (+) ⭐

Factor increasesReal rateMechanism
Expected real GDP growth ↑↑ Rises (+)Expect to be richer later → marginal utility of future consumption↓ → mₜ↓ → less incentive to save → must pay more interest
GDP growth volatility ↑↑ Rises (+)Greater uncertainty → higher risk premium demanded → dominates the precautionary-saving effect
⚠ Common trapHigher volatility pits precautionary saving (mₜ↑ → Rf↓) against the risk premium (Rf↑). The risk premium dominates, so the net relationship is positive (+). Concluding "negative" from precautionary saving alone is the trap.
