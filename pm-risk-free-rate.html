Risk-Free Rate in Portfolio TheoryThe baseline of the CAL, CML & CAPM

In one line — the risk-free asset has zero volatility and zero correlation, so combining it with a risky portfolio produces a perfectly straight risk–return line (the CML), and it becomes the starting point (intercept) of every pricing model (CAPM).

↔ Series linkThe Economics and Investment Markets · Real Risk-Free Rate note covered why a rate exists. This note covers how that rate is used in portfolio and asset-pricing models. Nominal Rf = real Rf + expected inflation — the Rf in the models below is that nominal figure.

1. Two properties of the risk-free asset (the starting point of everything)

In portfolio theory the risk-free asset is defined by exactly two mathematical properties. It is usually proxied by short-term government bills (T-bills).

① Standard deviation = 0  (σf = 0)
Its future return is certain → no volatility.

② Correlation with any risky asset = 0  (ρ = 0, Cov = 0)
Reason: Cov(f, i) = ρ · σf · σi, and σf = 0 forces the covariance to 0.

ⓘ Why it mattersThese two zeros mean that mixing the risk-free asset with any risky portfolio raises risk (σ) only linearly. That is exactly why you get a straight line, not a curve.

2. Risk-free + risky asset = a straight line (the CAL)

Put weight w in risky portfolio i and (1−w) in the risk-free asset.

E(Rp) = (1−w)·Rf + w·E(Ri) = Rf + w·[E(Ri) − Rf] // expected return

Expanding the variance (σf=0, Cov=0 kill the last two terms):

Var(Rp) = w²σi² + (1−w)²·σf²(=0) + 2w(1−w)·Cov(=0)
= w²σi² ⟹ σp = w·σi // risk is linear!

Substituting w = σp/σi into the expected return gives a straight line in σp:

E(Rp) = Rf + [ E(Ri) − Rf ] / σi · σp // this line is the CAL. slope = Sharpe ratio
  • Intercept = Rf — with zero risk (all in the risk-free asset) the return is Rf.
  • Slope = [E(Ri) − Rf] / σi = the Sharpe ratio — excess return per unit of risk; the "price of risk."

3. The best CAL = the Capital Market Line (CML)

Among all CALs, pick the one with the highest slope (Sharpe ratio). That line is tangent to the efficient frontier, and the tangency point is the market portfolio M. This optimal CAL is called the Capital Market Line (CML).

E(Rp) = Rf + [ E(Rm) − Rf ] / σm · σp // slope = market Sharpe ratio = price of market risk
Position on the CMLCompositionName
Between Rf and Mpart Rf + part M (wM < 100%)Lending conservative
Exactly at M100% market portfolioTangency portfolio
Beyond M (upper right)Borrow at Rf to buy >100% of M (leverage)Borrowing aggressive

4. Two-fund separation theorem

Regardless of risk preference, every investor splits money between just two things:

  • ① the risk-free asset (Rf)
  • ② the same market portfolio M (the identical tangency portfolio for everyone)

The only difference is the weight between the two funds. → The "investment decision (what to hold = M)" is separated from the "risk decision (how much = w)." Risk-averse → more in Rf; aggressive → leverage M by borrowing.

5. CAPM / SML — Rf becomes the intercept

Where the CML prices efficient portfolios by total risk (σ), the CAPM prices individual assets by systematic risk (β) alone. Its graph is the Security Market Line (SML).

E(Ri) = Rf + βi · [ E(Rm) − Rf ] // [ ] = market risk premium (MRP)
  • Intercept = Rf (the expected return when β = 0). The risk-free asset has β = 0.
  • Slope = market risk premium = E(Rm) − Rf
  • β = 1 → E(Ri) = E(Rm) (same as the market).
ⓘ ExampleRf = 4%, MRP = 5%, β = 1.2 → E(R) = 4% + 1.2×5% = 10%.

⚠ TrapBoth share the intercept Rf, but the x-axis and slope differ. "CML = σ and efficient portfolios; SML = β and all assets." Plotting a single stock on the CML is wrong.

6. Performance measures that use Rf

Rf is the "you could have earned this risk-free" hurdle, so it is the starting point for excess return.

MeasureFormulaRisk measure
Sharpe(Rp − Rf) / σptotal risk σ
Treynor(Rp − Rf) / βpsystematic β
M² (M-squared)(Rp−Rf)·(σm/σp) − (Rm−Rf)total risk σ (return units)
Jensen's αRp − [Rf + βp(Rm−Rf)]systematic β

Memory hook: denominator σ → total risk (Sharpe, M²); β → systematic risk (Treynor, Jensen). All four start from Rf-adjusted excess return.

7. Reality check — when the assumptions break

① Borrowing rate > lending rate
In practice investors cannot borrow at Rf; they borrow at a higher rate. → The CML kinks at M. Left of M is the steeper lending line; right of M is the flatter borrowing line.

② Which Rf in practice?
Short-horizon work → T-bills; cost of equity (long-horizon cash flows) → a long-term government bond yield matched to the horizon. The key is matching the rate's maturity to the cash-flow horizon.

③ Nominal vs real
Model inputs are usually the nominal Rf. When a real analysis is needed, decompose it as nominal = real + expected inflation (Fisher).

8. Summary flow

Risk-free asset: σf=0, Cov=0 (two zeros)

↓

↓

↓
